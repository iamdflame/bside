# B-Side — Demo Video Guide (do this exactly)

This is your step-by-step recipe to make a **3-minute demo video**. It is written so you never
have to guess. Read it once, then do it top to bottom.

You will:
1. Make the **voiceover** with ElevenLabs (I give you the voice + exact settings + the words).
2. Grab **clean copies** of one audiogram and the art (so the pretty parts look perfect).
3. **Record your screen** in short clips (I tell you exactly what to click).
4. **Edit in CapCut Pro on your phone** (I give you every tap).
5. **Check and upload.**

- **The live site:** https://bside-production.up.railway.app
- **Start page for the demo:** click **Judge mode** in the top menu (no sign-in needed).

> Golden rule: **the voiceover covers everything.** So your screen clips do **not** need sound.
> That makes recording easy — you can mess up and just re-record one little clip.

# PART A — Make the voiceover in ElevenLabs

### A1. The voice to use

- Open **ElevenLabs** and go to **Text to Speech** (also called the "Speech" or "Studio" page).
- In the voice picker, search for and pick: **Brian**
  - Brian is a warm, confident, grown-up American narrator. It sounds like a real documentary.
  - **Don't like it?** Good backups (search these names): **Bill** (older, trustworthy),
    **Adam** (classic deep), or if you want a woman's voice: **Alice** (British, crisp) or
    **Matilda** (warm American).
- Pick **one** voice and use it for **all six** clips so they match.

### A2. The model to use

- Set the model to **Eleven Multilingual v2** (best quality; this is not live, so quality wins).
  - If your account only shows **Eleven v3** or **Turbo**, that's OK — Multilingual v2 is just the
    safest. Any of them will sound good with the settings below.

### A3. The exact settings (set these once — they stay set)

Move the sliders to:

- **Speed:** `1.0` (normal)
- **Stability:** `50%` (half-way — steady but still human)
- **Similarity:** `80%`
- **Style Exaggeration:** `0%` (keep it natural for narration)
- **Speaker Boost:** **ON**

That's it. These give a calm, clear, trustworthy read.

### A4. How to make each clip

You will generate **6 separate clips**. Doing them one at a time keeps the pauses clean and makes
editing easy.

For each clip:
1. **Delete** whatever is in the text box.
2. **Paste one clip's words** (from A6 below).
3. Press **Generate**.
4. Listen. If a word sounds weird, press Generate again (it varies slightly each time) or fix the
   spelling using the pronunciation tips in A5.
5. Press **Download** to save the MP3.
6. **Rename** the file so you don't get lost:
   `clip1.mp3`, `clip2.mp3`, `clip3.mp3`, `clip4.mp3`, `clip5.mp3`, `clip6.mp3`.

Save all six into one folder (or your phone's Files app) — you'll need them in CapCut.

### A5. Pronunciation tips (so it says things right)

If any of these sound off, type them this way in the box, then change them back only in your head:

- **B2** → type it as **"B-two"**
- **B-Side** → type **"B-side"**
- **Genblaze** → type **"Gen-blaze"**
- **Backblaze** → type **"Back-blaze"**
- **audiogram** → usually fine; if not, type **"audio-gram"**
- **re-hash** → type **"re-hash"** (with the dash)

### A6. The words to paste (your script — 6 clips)

**Clip 1 — Hook**
> Every podcaster knows the secret. Making the episode is the easy part. The transcript, the show
> notes, the art, the clips — that's the three hours nobody talks about. This is B-side. Watch it
> do all of it. Live.

**Clip 2 — Drop and start**
> You drop in your audio, and the pipeline starts on its own. Ingest. Transcribe. Every step you
> see saves its files to Back-blaze B-two first — through Gen-blaze — before it moves on. Nothing
> is faked. This is really running.

**Clip 3 — The kit arrives**
> In about two minutes, the whole kit is back. Three titles. Chapters with timestamps. Show notes.
> Cover art — checked by a quality gate that already threw away the bad frames. Quote cards. And
> the one that matters most for growth: audiograms. That's the creator's real voice… with captions
> that light up word by word, pulled straight from the transcript's timing. This is composed. Not

**Clip 4 — Your call**

# PART B — Grab clean copies of the pretty parts

Your screen recording is great for showing clicks. But for the **audiogram** and the **art**, a
clean original file looks and sounds perfect. This takes 2 minutes and makes the video pop.

**Easiest way — download the kit ZIP:**
1. On the live site, click **Judge mode**.
2. Under **"2 · Or inspect a finished kit,"** click any episode that says **sealed**.
3. On that page, click the blue **"Download kit v1 ↓"** button (top right).
4. You get a **.zip** file. Open it. Inside you'll find:
   - the **audiogram** videos (`.mp4`) — pick the one you like best
   - the **episode art** (`.png`)
   - the **quote cards** (`.png`)
5. Save the **one audiogram mp4** and the **art png** to your phone (or send them to your phone).

**Even faster on a computer (optional):** on the episode page, **right-click the audiogram video →
"Save video as…"**, and **right-click the art → "Save image as…"**.

Keep these two files with your voice clips. You'll drop them into CapCut for the close-up moments.

# PART C — Record your screen (short clips)

### C1. Which device to record on

**Best-looking option: record on a computer.** The pipeline page shows a nice side rail there. You
then send the clips to your phone (AirDrop, Google Drive, or a cable) to edit in CapCut.

- **Mac:** press **Cmd + Shift + 5** → choose **Record Selected Portion** or **Entire Screen** →
  **Record**. Stop from the top menu bar.
- **Windows:** press **Win + G** (Game Bar) → click the **Record** (circle) button. Or use the
  Snipping Tool's record feature.

**Only have a phone? That's fine.** Turn the phone **sideways (landscape)** and use the built-in
screen recorder:
- **iPhone:** Settings → Control Center → add **Screen Recording**. Then swipe down and tap the
  round record button.
- **Android:** swipe down → **Screen Record** (turn **sound: on / internal** if it asks).

> Remember: sound doesn't matter here. The voiceover covers it. Just get clean **picture**.

### C2. Get ready (2-minute prep before recording)

1. Open the live site and make the browser window clean (close extra tabs; hide bookmarks bar).
2. Click **Judge mode**.
3. Click **"Run the pipeline now →"** once. This starts a fresh episode. Let it run in the
   background — you'll use it for the "watch it move" clip and it'll be finished for later clips.
4. Make sure there's at least **one sealed episode** in the list (there already is). If your fresh
   run ever fails (free provider limits), just use a **sealed** one for the kit and download shots.

### C3. Record these clips, one at a time

Record each as its own short clip. Re-do any that feel messy — it's just one clip.

**Clip A — Open (for shot 1):**
1. Start recording.
2. Show the **home page** for 2 seconds.
3. Click **Judge mode** in the top menu.
4. Let the Judge page sit for 2 seconds.
5. Stop. *(about 8 seconds)*

**Clip B — It runs (for shot 2):**
1. Start recording on the Judge page.
2. Click **"Run the pipeline now →"**.
3. It jumps to the episode page. Watch the left **Pipeline** rail: **Ingest** turns green, then
   **Transcribe**, then **Direct** starts.
4. Record about **30–40 seconds** of the dots turning green, then stop. *(You'll speed this up
   later, so it feels instant.)*

**Clip C — The kit (for shot 3):**
1. Open a **finished** episode (from the Judge list, one that says **in review** or **sealed**).
2. Start recording.
3. Slowly scroll down: show **Direction** (titles, chapters), then **Episode art**, then the
   **Quote cards**, then an **audiogram**.
4. **Click play on one audiogram** and let it play for ~5 seconds (captions lighting up).
5. Stop. *(about 25–30 seconds)*

> The audiogram close-up will use your **clean mp3/mp4** from Part B, so even if the screen version
> stutters, your final video won't.

**Clip D — Reject and regenerate (for shot 4):**
1. On an episode that is **in review**, find the **Episode art** with **Approve / Reject** buttons.
2. Start recording.
3. Click **Reject**. A box appears.
4. Type: `more luminous, deep blue with a coral signal path` and click **Reject & regenerate**.
5. Show the art area (it will start regenerating).
6. Then click **⛨ verify** on any asset to open the **evidence drawer**. Show the
   **"Parent run (lineage)"** line.
7. Stop. *(about 25 seconds)*

**Clip E — The proof (for shot 5):**
1. Keep the **evidence drawer** open (click **⛨ verify** on the art if it's closed).
2. Start recording.
3. Click **"Fetch bytes & re-hash now"**.
4. Wait ~1 second for the green **✓ BYTES MATCH** box. Hold on it for 3 seconds.
5. Close the drawer. Go to **Judge mode**.
6. Click **"Restore state from B2"**. Show the green **"✓ rebuilt … from B2"** line.
7. Stop. *(about 25 seconds)*

**Clip F — Download (for shot 6):**
1. Open a **sealed** episode.
2. Start recording.
3. Click **"Download kit v1 ↓"** (top right).
4. Show that a file downloads. Stop. *(about 8 seconds)*

Now you have **6 screen clips (A–F)** + **6 voice clips (clip1–clip6)** + **1 clean audiogram** +
**1 clean art**. Send everything to your phone.

# PART D — Edit in CapCut Pro (on your phone)

Open **CapCut** and follow these taps. Don't worry about perfection — go in order.

### D1. Start the project
1. Tap **New project**.
2. Select your screen clips **A, B, C, D, E, F** (in that order) and the **clean audiogram mp4**.
   Tap **Add**.
3. They land on the timeline (the strip at the bottom).

### D2. Put them in the right order
- Drag clips so the order is: **A → B → C → D → E → F**.
- You'll slot the clean audiogram **inside** shot 3 in step D6.

### D3. Add the voiceover (your ElevenLabs clips)
1. Tap an empty part of the timeline (so nothing is selected).
2. Tap **Audio** (bottom bar) → **Sounds**.
3. Tap the **device / local files** option (a phone or folder icon, sometimes called **"Local"**
   or **"Your sounds"**). If you don't see it, first move the mp3s into your phone's **Files** or
   **Music** app — then they show up here.
4. Add **clip1.mp3**. It drops onto an audio track.
5. Repeat for **clip2 … clip6**, one after another, in order.

### D4. Line up the voice with the picture
- Drag each voice clip so it **starts where its shot starts**:
  - clip1 under shot A
  - clip2 under shot B
  - clip3 under shot C
  - clip4 under shot D
  - clip5 under shot E
  - clip6 under shot F
- Tip: the voiceover is the boss. If a shot is **too short** for its voice clip, do D5 to stretch
  it; if a shot is **too long**, trim its end by dragging the clip edge inward.

### D5. Speed up the boring waiting parts (this is the magic)
Your "it runs" clip (B) is ~35 seconds but should feel like ~10.
1. Tap clip **B**.
2. Tap **Speed** → **Normal**.
3. Drag to about **3x–5x**. Now the green dots race across. 
4. Do the same to any clip that feels slow (like long scrolls in C).
- Leave the **BYTES MATCH** moment (E) and the **audiogram** (D6) at **normal speed** — those are
  the "wow" beats; don't rush them.

### D6. Drop in the clean audiogram (the money shot)
1. Move the playhead to the middle of shot **C**, right where the voice says
   *"that's the creator's real voice."*
2. Tap the screen clip C, then tap **Split** at the playhead (scissors), so C is cut in two.
3. Tap the empty spot → **Add** (the "+" on the timeline) → import the **clean audiogram mp4** and
   place it **between** the two halves of C.
4. Tap the audiogram clip → set its **volume**: tap **Volume** and turn it **up to ~100%** so you
   hear the real voice + captions for ~5 seconds. (Your voiceover clip3 keeps playing under it —
   see D8 to duck it.)

### D7. Add big words on screen (optional but classy)
1. Tap **Text** → **Add text**.
2. Type the line from the shot list (e.g., `Re-checked live from Backblaze B2`).
3. Pick a clean font, white color, and drag it to the **lower third** of the screen.
4. Drag its ends so it shows for ~2–3 seconds over the right shot.
5. Repeat for the other lines you want (keep it to 4–5 total — don't overdo it).

**Bonus (accessibility points):** tap **Text → Auto captions → your voiceover track → turn on**.
CapCut writes subtitles for your narration automatically. Quickly fix any wrong words.

### D8. Add background music (quietly)
1. Tap **Audio → Sounds** → search a calm, royalty-free track (try **"minimal"**, **"corporate"**,
   or **"lo-fi"**). Use **CapCut's own library only** so there are no copyright problems.
2. Add it at the start.
3. Tap the music → **Volume** → set it **low (about 10–15%)** so the voice stays clear.
4. Tap **Fade** → add a short **fade in** and **fade out**.
5. **Duck under the audiogram:** during your clean audiogram (D6), lower the music even more (or
   split it and set that piece to ~5%) so the real voice shines.

### D9. Smooth the cuts
- Tap the small **white box between two clips** → pick a **simple** transition (a quick **Fade** or
  **Pull in**). Keep it short (0.2–0.3s). Don't use flashy ones.

### D10. Export
1. Top-right: tap **1080P**, set **30 fps** (60 is fine too), **quality High**.
2. Tap **Export**. Wait for it to save to your gallery.
3. If CapCut offers a **"Copyright check,"** run it to be safe.

## If something goes wrong (quick fixes)

- **A live run failed** (free provider limit hit): don't panic. Use an **already-sealed** episode
  for shots C, D, E, F. Only shot B needs live motion, and even a partial run (Ingest + Transcribe
  going green) is enough.
- **The audiogram looks choppy in the screen recording:** ignore it — you're using the **clean mp4**
  from Part B for that close-up anyway.
- **CapCut won't find your mp3s:** move them into the phone's **Files** app (a normal folder), then
  re-open **Audio → Sounds → Local**.
- **Voice says a word weird:** re-generate that one clip, or spell it like in **A5**.
- **Site shows a quick error right after you redeploy:** wait 60 seconds and refresh — it's just the
  server swapping in the new version.

You've got everything. Make the six voice clips first, grab the clean audiogram, record the six
short screen clips, then assemble in CapCut. Take your time on the **audiogram** and the
**BYTES MATCH** — those two beats win the video.
